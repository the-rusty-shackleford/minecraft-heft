# D-0003 — The class canon

**The ask.** nfx: "every ingot is the same ingot geometry with a different texture... If iron and
copper ingots have visibly different proportions, that's a bug." The handoff's §4.3 proposes a canon
per class (ingot 10 x 3 x 5, nugget, raw chunk, gem, shard, dust pile, lump, rod, plate, book, bar,
loaf, sign).

**Decision.**

- **One function per class** in `devtools/art/shapes.py`, taking nothing but the material. Every
  item of a class gets the same element list. `tests/test_shapes.py` reads every built shape file and
  fails if two items of one class differ in geometry.
- **Every model is centred on the block's centre (8, 8, 8)**, as the game's own flat items are, so
  display transforms read like the game's. The handoff's "resting on y = 0" serves nothing that
  measures a model: Set It Down sizes and places by the measured box.
- **The `fixed` turn is the rest pose.** Set It Down's `HeldView.Request.pose` uses `Rest.of` only
  where the hand draws another model than the item's own (a pack's held model: its D-0005). A
  Heft model is the item's own in every view, so Set It Down lays it down by its own `fixed`
  rotation, then sizes it from the measured box. The handoff's §4.1-4.2 ("display.fixed is not what
  positions it") describes the other path. So:
  - a class that lies on its base (ingot, gem, dust, lump, plate) has `fixed` = [-90, 0, 0], which
    turns its top (+y) out of the surface, towards the viewer in a frame;
  - every class's `fixed` turn lays its thinnest extent out of the surface. The domain's
    `Canon.breaches` holds this in the game, reported after every bake, and
    `tests/test_shapes.py` holds it in the generator.
- **The element budget is 40** (`Canon.BUDGET`), reported after every bake.
- **The ingot (phase 0)**: three courses (10 x 1 x 5, 9 x 1 x 4, 8 x 1 x 3), a taper stepped half a
  sixteenth each side, so each course's rim catches light. Its look is a placeholder until phase 1
  is judged in photos.
- **Isometric `gui` for material classes** (the handoff's §8.1(b), adopted): [30, 225, 0].

**Phase 1, settled by preview and the booth (2026-10-25).**
- **The ingot is a bar cut on its four long edges**, a sixteenth at the top and a quarter at the foot,
  so its section is a trapezoid, wider below. The handoff's §4.3 asks for "a chamfer on the four long
  edges". Cutting the ends too left a notch at every corner (boxes cannot close a hip), so the ends are
  square. The nugget is the same form at 4 x 2 x 3, the brick at 8 x 3 x 4 cut evenly.
- **Round things are octagonal tiers**, four slabs each, by the handoff's R cos 22.5 by R sin 22.5 rule:
  the gem (pavilion, girdle, crown, table) and the dust mound (three tiers, each set off the last). The
  shard is a diamond section tapering in three steps. The raw chunk and the lump are clusters turned
  22.5 degrees.
- **Each class has a `fill`**, its size among the others, and every display scale is worked from the
  shape's own extent times it, so a nugget is small in its slot and an ingot fills it.
- **Held in first person it lies across the palm** ([10, -20, 5]), 4.5 sixteenths across. Pointed away
  at [0, 45, 0], an ingot looked like a baton. **Left-hand entries equal the right's**: the game's
  `ItemTransform.apply` mirrors a left-hand transform itself.

**Phase 3, settled by preview and the booth (2026-10-25).**
- **Rod** 11 x 1.5 x 1.5 along x, its ends stepped in; **plate** 11 x 0.5 x 9, corners cut by two crossed
  sheets a hair apart, one corner curled 22.5 degrees; **book** 10 x 2.5 x 7, covers overhanging the
  page block half a sixteenth; **arrow** a shaft, a two-bladed head and a point, two crossed vanes.
  Plates and books show more of their top in the inventory ([50, 225, 0]).
- **Signs stand face on** (`lies: face_out`, `fixed` [0, 180, 0], as a flat item's): a board 14 x 8 x
  1.5 on a post, or under a bar on two chains. Their hands, ground and frame use the game's own flat-item
  transforms scaled to the board's width; the inventory turns them a little ([10, -25, 0]).
- **A material may have parts**: a role made of another material (a book's pages are paper, a sign's
  chains iron, an arrow's head flint and its fletching feather), each with its own F0.
- **Tint**: faces of the role `head` carry tint index 0, so a tipped arrow's head takes its potion's
  colour (the game's item colours tint layer 0 of a tipped arrow).

**Phase 4, food, settled by preview and the booth (2026-10-25).** Our own canon, not a Fresh Food port
(the handoff's §8.3, adopted).
- **Sixteen classes over 82 items**, vanilla's and Farmer's Delight's (`devtools/art/food.py`): fruit
  (body in three octagonal tiers, stem, leaf), egg, root (diamond-section segments tapering, a tuft),
  tuber, loaf (a deep cut along its crown), disc (cookie), pie, pie slice, steak (raw and cooked pairs
  share it), drumstick (its bone a part), fish and melon wedge (standing face on, as a sign does), berries,
  bowl, plated meal, sandwich stack.
- **The bowl is hollow**: a ring of eight tangential segments, each a side of the octagon (four square,
  four turned 45 degrees about the centre), what is in it set half a sixteenth below the rim.
- **Each food's colour is measured from its own sprite**, numbers only. The painter can now keep a hue
  band of a palette (a carrot's orange without its leaves, a melon's rind green and flesh red) and
  subtract another class's palette (a stew without its bowl's browns, a meal without its dish).
- Left to the long tail: Farmer's Delight's drinks (bottles and mugs), cake, the milk bucket, and FD's
  non-food items (knives, canvas signs, wild crops, mats).

**Rusty's review answers (2026-10-26).**
- **The bone is a class of its own** (it was a rod, the handoff's canon, and read as a pale stick): a shaft 1.5
  thick flaring at each end into two knobs side by side, 12 x 2 x 5 lying flat, each end's knobs turned an eighth
  and mirrored so they splay apart alike.
- **Coal and brass lifted.** Their colours are their sprites' own, numbers only, so the change is in which of the
  measured colours a material keeps and how it is moved. A material can multiply its kept colours' value
  (`gain`) and saturation (`chroma`): coal and Subterranean Wilds' coal shard at 1.9 and 0.35, so their faces part
  without the purple cast the lift brought up out of the sprite's near-blacks. Brass keeps the middle of Create's
  palette (0.25 to 0.5) rather than its upper half, which was mostly a near-white yellow. Charcoal, whose sprite
  has a lighter end, is unchanged.
- **Fresh Food keeps its own 3D** (D-0007 as written).


**Drawn over Fresh Food (D-0008, 2026-10-26).** The **leg** (mutton, raw and cooked): a whole joint, 12.5 x 4.4 x
6.4, full at the rump and narrowing to the shank, its bone a part run out to a knuckle. The **chop** (pork, raw
and cooked): a round of meat 6.4 across in a rim of fat 8.4 across and 1.6 thick, the meat showing on both faces a
hair proud, the bone out of its side; roles `chop_meat` at tone 0.06 (the darkest kept colour, where vanilla's
cooked chop has its golden brown) and `fat` at 0.88. The **tuber** is now two rounded lumps run together, three
octagonal tiers each, so a potato reads as an oblong with a dip rather than a log.
