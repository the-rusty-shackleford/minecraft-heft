# D-0010 — A relief per class, and where the solid model is drawn

**The ask.** Rusty, on D-0009's review (2026-10-27): the live models "are better overall and going the right
direction", but some look over-inflated: "Bread looks like someone blew it up with air. Don't treat every round
item the same way. Shape each one from what it actually is: a loaf has a flat bottom, a domed crust and squared
ends; a fruit is round; a cookie is a thin disc; a steak is a slab." Then, on the first set of reliefs: "a lot of
these things ... look like plushies or balloons ... just a little too puffy"; and "keep the RELATIVE sizes of
things from vanilla". And: "keep the vanilla/pack sprite in the inventory, hotbar and any other GUI slot. Use the
new 3D models only in the hand (first and third person), for Set It Down, and for dropped items." Asked about a
placed item frame, he chose **the flat sprite, like a slot**.

**Decision.**

- **Reliefs** (`domain/Relief`, JDK only, unit-tested): an item is raised from its live sprite's silhouette by a
  relief chosen by what it is, front and back each by its own section. A side's height at distance d from the
  silhouette's edge is `min(rel * r, cap) * shoulder(d) * body(d / r)`: r the inradius, the body the section across
  the whole width (round, dome, cone or flat, weighted by `crown`), the shoulder a parabola over the first `edge`
  sixteenths (squared ends). Sixteenths of the sprite's square, so a 128x sprite is shaped as its 16x.
  - sphere (fruit, berries, egg, orb, spawn egg), loaf (flat bottom, domed crust, squared ends), disc (cookie),
    slab (steak, chop), wedge (melon slice), pie and plated (low on a flat bottom), mound (dust, minced beef),
    lump (coal, raw ore, nugget), tuber, tube (carrot, leg, drumstick, bone), gem (cut to a ridge: gem, shard),
    fish (round, flatter), bowl, shell (domed over a flat underside), bar (ingot, brick: level, bevelled), stack
    (sandwich), thin (paper, hide: no thicker than the game's own, its outline smoothed). Signs, books, records,
    rods, arrows and the rest are extruded.
  - **Not puffy**: every body stands lower than its round shape would (rel 0.3 to 0.5 of the inradius), its top
    fuller (crown under 1), one smoothing pass.
  - Where a class is too broad, an item has its own (minced beef a mound, beetroot a tuber); a food with no class
    goes by a word of its name (catfish a fish, sweet roll a loaf, bratwurst a tube), else a modest rounded `food`.
  - The back is what a set-down item rests on (Set It Down lays an item face up), so the flat-bottomed reliefs lie flat.
  - Every spawn egg, any mod's, is raised as a sphere from its own two tinted layers; another mod's own egg models
    (VanillaBackport's, drawn past the item renderer's choice) are raised too, flat in a slot or frame by the view.
- **Sizes are vanilla's**: every model is drawn by its flat original's transforms, so items stand to each other as
  they do in the game.
- **Where the solid model is drawn**: held (first and third person), dropped, set down, worn on the head. **Flat** (the
  model Heft made it from, the pack's): every GUI slot (`GuiGraphics.renderItem`'s look-up, so its lighting is the flat
  item's, and the GUI view itself, for anything drawing a slot another way) and an item frame (`ItemFrameRenderer`
  says when it draws; the FIXED view is otherwise solid, so Set It Down, which also measures in FIXED, is untouched).
  D-0008's swap and the draw-time path are skipped in those views too: a slot or frame shows exactly what the pack shows.

**Alternatives rejected.** *One parabola for every round thing* (the first prototype: "blown up with air").
*A per-item hand-tuned depth* (hundreds of numbers; the class is what the thing is). *Each item as large as its
0.1.0 shape* (Rusty: vanilla's relative sizes). *Set It Down raising a flag while it draws* (a patch to a second
mod; the frame's own renderer says the same).
